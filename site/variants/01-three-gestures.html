Takes Source Download

Dictation and video takes for the Mac Local. Free. MIT. macOS 14, Apple Silicon

Three gestures. That is the whole interface.

  1. 1

    Hold Option. Speak. Let go.

    Hold ⌥ Left Option

    The words are pasted at your cursor, in any app that has one. Audio never leaves the machine.

  2. 2

    Double tap Option. Speak. Tap once to finish.

    Tap twice ⌥ Left Option

    A locked take, for narration that runs longer than you want to hold a key. Tap the trigger to paste. Esc throws it away.

    Inside a locked take, hold the trigger again and add Shift for shots or clips.

  3. 3

    Hold Option and Shift. Let go to stop.

    Hold ⌥ Left Option + ⇧ Shift

    The screen and the microphone are recorded while you hold. On release they are packaged into a folder an agent can read, and the path is pasted at your cursor.

The fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it.

01

155 ms, on the CPU, with nothing uploaded

The speech model is NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6b v3, quantised to INT8 and run through ONNX on the CPU. On an M3 Max a six-second Swedish sentence comes back in about 155 ms. That is faster than reaching for the mouse.

Nothing is uploaded, ever. The 670 MB model downloads once, on first launch, and lives in ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/models/. After that the app works on a plane.

02

A take is a folder an agent can read

Hold the trigger with Shift and talk through what is on screen. Let go. The recording is written to ~/Documents/see.computer/ under a timestamp, and a short paste lands at your cursor. That paste is the narration in quotes, a blank line, then the names of the captures and the path to take.md.

An agent that can read files opens the exact frames where each thing was said, without decoding the video. A human, or a web chatbox, still gets the words. Both get the same paste.

Releasing before about 0.6 seconds discards the take, so a slipped key does not leave a file behind.

03

Cleanup happens before the paste

Spoken text is not written text. Three passes run between the model and the cursor. None of them need a network.

Hesitation

um, uh and hm are dropped, together with the comma or full stop stuck to them. The next word takes over the capital. "like" and "actually" stay, because they are usually real words. "er" stays, because it is Swedish for "your". A dictation that was only a hum pastes nothing.

Vocabulary

Put words the model misses in ~/Documents/see.computer/vocabulary.md, one per line. They are pushed into the decoder while it is still choosing, not substituted afterwards. The file is re-read when it changes. No restart.

A term only gets its bonus when the model was already considering it, so a long list stays safe. A bonus can win a close call. It cannot overturn a confident one.

04

The pill is the only thing on screen

A small overlay at the bottom of the display. Cyan bars while listening, scaled to the loudest thing heard lately, so a quiet room and a loud one both look alive. Red while recording. A lit ring when a take is locked, so a live microphone never looks idle.

If nothing could take the paste, because no text field had focus, the pill stretches into a card with the text and a Copy button. Nothing is lost.

Motion is a spring, 0.3 s response and 0.7 damping.

Listening

Trigger held. Bars follow the microphone.

Recording

Trigger and Shift held. Screen and voice captured.

Locked

Double tapped. Hands free. Tap to paste, Esc to discard.

Move the submit button above the table.

Nothing focused

No cursor to paste into. The text waits here instead.

05

Tray, history, trigger

The menu bar panel lists recent dictations and recordings. Click one to copy it again. Settings live in the same panel, and there are few of them.

Dictation history is one markdown file per day in ~/Documents/see.computer/. Plain files. Read them or delete them. Nothing prunes them for you. Turn the whole thing off with "Save Dictation History" in Settings.

The audio-never-leaves promise covers audio. If iCloud Desktop & Documents sync is on, the transcript files will sync like any other document.

Trigger
Left Option, Right Option, or Fn, the Globe key. Right Option is AltGr on the Swedish layout. Recording and locking follow whichever trigger you picked.

06

Four permissions, and why each one

macOS will ask for these on first use. Each one maps to one thing the app does. Screen Recording is only requested when you first use the video gesture.

Accessibility
To press Cmd+V for you. That is how the text reaches the cursor.
Input Monitoring
To notice a bare modifier key being held down. That is what hold-to-talk is.
Microphone
To hear you. Audio is transcribed on the CPU and never uploaded.
Screen Recording
For the video gesture only. Capture goes through the system screencapture tool.
07

Requirements and build

macOS 14 or later on Apple Silicon. Download the DMG, drag it to Applications, and hold Option.

Built with Tauri v2 and Rust. One controller thread owns the app state. The menu bar panel is native AppKit with NSGlassEffectView. There is no bundler and no Node toolchain, so a build is one cargo command.

From source

cd src-tauri
cargo tauri build --debug --bundles app

Command line, inside the bundle

see-computer transcribe <wav>
