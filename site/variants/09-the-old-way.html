Free, open source, Mac only

The old way took eleven steps.

see.computer is the fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it. Speak, and the words land at your cursor. Hold Shift too, and the screen and your voice are captured into a folder an agent can read.

Download for Mac View source macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon. MIT.
11 steps

Showing an agent your screen, before

  1. Press CmdShift4.
  2. Drag a box around the thing that is wrong.
  3. Wait for the thumbnail to slide off the corner of the screen.
  4. Open Finder. Find the file among the other screenshots on the Desktop.
  5. Drag it into the chat box.
  6. Type what the picture is supposed to show.
  7. Realise the interesting part happened after you took the shot.
  8. Press CmdShift5 and record it again as a clip.
  9. Stop. Find the .mov. Learn that the chat box will not take video.
  10. Describe in words what happened, and at which second.
  11. Send it. Answer the follow-up asking which frame you meant.
3 steps

With see.computer

  1. Hold OptionShift.
  2. Say what you see.
  3. Release.

On release the screen recording, the microphone audio and the transcript are packaged into a take folder. What gets pasted is your words in quotes, a blank line, then the names of the captures and the path to take.md. Let go before about 0.6 seconds and nothing is saved.

Speed and privacy
155milliseconds for a six-second Swedish sentence on an M3 Max

The speech model runs on your CPU. NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6b v3, quantised to INT8 and run through ONNX. Nothing is uploaded, so there is nothing to wait for and nothing to leak.

The model is 670 MB and downloads once, on first launch, into ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/models/. Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. The README shows English and Swedish.

Old

Your voice goes to a server. You wait for the round trip. You trust the terms of service.

New

Your voice stays on the laptop. The text is back before you have let go of the key.

For agents

A folder an agent can read

Every recording lands in ~/Documents/see.computer/ as a take. The video is there, and so are JPEG frames, so an agent that can read files opens the exact frames where each thing was said without decoding the video. A human or a web chat box still gets the words.

What is in a take

  • ~/Documents/see.computer/<stamp>/
    • take.md the narration, with what was captured when
    • transcript.json
    • shots/ screenshots
    • clips/
      • 001/
        • clip.mov
        • frames/ JPEG frames from the clip

Cleanup

Tidied before it lands

You talk like a person. The paste reads like you typed it.

Hesitation is dropped

Um, so the, uh, button.→So the button.

um, uh and hm go, together with the comma or full stop stuck to them, and the next word takes over the capital. like and actually stay because they are usually real words. er stays because it is Swedish for "your". A dictation that was only a hum pastes nothing.

New

They never arrive.

Your words, in the decoder

Put words the model misses in ~/Documents/see.computer/vocabulary.md, one per line. They are pushed into the decoder while it is still choosing, not substituted afterwards. The file is re-read on change, no restart.

A term only gets its bonus when the model was already considering it, so a long list stays safe. A bonus can win a close call. It cannot overturn a confident one.

The pill

You always know what the mic is doing

A small overlay sits at the bottom of the screen. It has four states and none of them is ambiguous.

Listening

Cyan bars, scaled to the loudest thing heard lately, so a quiet room still shows movement.

Recording

The bars turn red while the screen is being recorded.

Locked

A lit ring while a take is locked. A live mic never looks idle.

Nowhere to paste

Move the retry into the catch block and log the attempt count. Copy

No focused text field? The pill stretches into a card with the text and a Copy button.

Menu bar

The tray, the history and the trigger

Tray panel

Lists recent dictations and recordings. Click one to copy it again. Settings hold the folder, launch at login, the history toggle, the trigger choice and shortcuts to the permission panes.

Old

Scroll back through the chat to find what you said.

New

Click it in the tray. It is on the clipboard.

History

One markdown file per day in ~/Documents/see.computer/. Plain files you can read, grep or delete. No auto-pruning. Turn it off with "Save Dictation History" in Settings.

Honest caveat. The audio never leaves the machine. The transcript files do, if iCloud Desktop & Documents sync is on.

New

Permissions

What macOS will ask for, and why

Four prompts. Each one maps to a thing the app does, and the tray links straight to the pane.

Accessibility

To press Cmd+V for you. That is how the text lands at the cursor.

Input Monitoring

To notice a bare modifier being held with nothing else pressed. Regular hotkeys cannot see that.

Microphone

To hear you. The audio is transcribed on the CPU and never uploaded.

Screen Recording

To record the screen when you hold the trigger with Shift. Dictation alone never asks for it.

Video hotkey only

How it is built

  • Tauri v2 and Rust. No bundler, no Node toolchain.
  • One controller thread owns the app state.
  • The menu bar panel is native AppKit with NSGlassEffectView.
  • Screen recording uses the system screencapture tool.

Inside the bundle

Two commands for scripts and for checking the model without the app in the way.

see-computer transcribe <wav>

see-computer clip <mov>
