dictation and takes for the Mac

Show your agent. Stop describing your screen.

see.computer is the fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it. Speak, and the words land at your cursor. Hold Shift too, and the screen and your voice are captured into a folder an agent can read.

Free and open source, MIT. macOS 14 or later, Apple Silicon. Audio never leaves the machine.

gestures

One key. Three things it can do.

The trigger is Left Option by default. Hold it to talk. Double tap it to lock the mic open. Add Shift and the screen comes along. Nothing to open, no window to find.

01 hold ⌥ option speak, release

Dictate

Text is pasted at the cursor in whatever app has focus. A terminal, a chat box, a commit message.

Transcription runs on the CPU. The audio never leaves the machine.

typing out a paragraph you could have said in eight seconds.

02 double tap ⌥⌥ speak, tap once to finish

Locked take

For narration that runs two minutes, so you are not pinning a key the whole time. Tap the trigger to finish and paste. Esc throws it away.

Inside a locked take, hold the trigger again and add Shift for shots or clips. A lit ring around the pill means the mic is live.

splitting a long walkthrough into six short dictations.

03 hold ⌥+⇧ shift release to stop

Record a take

Screen and microphone are recorded while held. On release the recording is packaged into a take folder and the paste lands as narration, a blank line, the capture names, and the path to take.md.

Release before about 0.6 seconds and the take is discarded.

speed and locality

About 155 ms, on the CPU, on your machine.

The speech model is NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6b v3 as INT8 ONNX. It runs on the CPU, so the GPU stays free for whatever you are building. On an M3 Max a six-second Swedish sentence comes back in about 155 ms. The 670 MB model downloads once on first launch. After that there is no network in the loop.

takes

A take is a folder, not a video.

Agents read files. They do not decode QuickTime. So a take is laid out for a reader with a file tool and nothing else. The narration is in take.md, the word timings are in transcript.json, every screenshot is a PNG, and every clip is already split into JPEG frames. The agent opens the exact frame where you said "this one" without touching the .mov.

  • take.mdThe narration and the captures in one file. Point an agent here and it has the whole story.
  • transcript.jsonWord timings, so a reader can line up "the second field" with the frame on screen at that moment.
  • shots/Screenshots as PNG. Taken with Shift inside a take.
  • clips/001/frames/JPEG frames of the clip. No video decoding on the agent's side. The .mov sits next to them for a human.

Where the paste goes matters less than you would think. A coding agent with file access follows the path. A web chat box that cannot open your disk still gets the words in quotes. Both are the same paste.

cleanup

What gets fixed before it pastes.

Speech has hums and spoken numbers and project names the model has never seen. Each of those is handled before Cmd+V, so the agent reads a sentence and not a transcript.

Hesitation is dropped

um, uh and hm go, along with the comma or full stop stuck to them, and the next word takes over the capital. "like" and "actually" stay because they are usually real words. "er" stays because it is Swedish for "your". A dictation that was only a hum pastes nothing.

Your words, inside the decoder

Put the words the model misses in ~/Documents/see.computer/vocabulary.md, one per line. They are pushed into the decoder while it is still choosing, not swapped in afterwards. A term only gets its bonus when the model was already considering it, so a long list stays safe. A bonus can win a close call, not overturn a confident one. The file is re-read on change, no restart.

the pill

One chip at the bottom of the screen says what the mic is doing.

It appears when you hold the trigger and goes away when you let go. Cyan bars scaled to the loudest thing heard lately. Red while the screen is recording. A ring when a take is locked, so a live mic never looks idle.

Listening

Cyan bars follow your level. Quiet room, short bars. The scale tracks the loudest recent sound, so a soft voice still reads as alive.

Recording

Red while Option+Shift is held and the screen is being captured. Let go and the take is packaged.

Locked

A lit ring means a locked take is running and the mic is open. Tap the trigger to finish, Esc to discard.

tray, history, triggers

Everything you said is a file you can read.

The menu bar panel lists recent dictations and recordings. Click one to copy it again. Settings are in the same panel and there are only a few of them.

History
One markdown file per day in ~/Documents/see.computer/. Plain text. Read it, grep it, delete it. Nothing is pruned for you. Turn it off with "Save Dictation History".
Trigger
Left Option, Right Option, or Fn (Globe). Right Option is AltGr on the Swedish layout. Recording and locking always follow whichever key you picked.

permissions

Four prompts from macOS, and why each one.

An app that pastes text and reads a bare modifier key needs more than the average menu bar utility. Here is the list, so nothing in the first-launch dance is a surprise.

AccessibilityTo press Cmd+V for you. That is the whole paste mechanism.
Input MonitoringTo see a modifier key held on its own. Option by itself is not a shortcut macOS will hand to an app any other way.
MicrophoneTo hear you. Audio is transcribed on the CPU and never uploaded.
Screen RecordingOnly for the Option+Shift take. Dictation works without it. Capture uses the system screencapture tool.optional
One caveat on "never leaves the machine". That promise covers audio. Transcripts and takes are ordinary files in ~/Documents. If iCloud Desktop & Documents sync is on, Apple will sync them like any other file. Turn off history, move the folder, or leave sync off if that matters to you.
