Ten landing pages for see.computer

Each page carries the full feature walk-through. They differ in the idea that leads and in how they look. Click a card to open the page at full size.

01

Three gestures. That is the whole interface.

The gesture table is the hero, set enormous. Near-monochrome paper and ink, hairline rules, one cyan accent. The rest of the page is numbered spec rows in the same grid, like a well-set specification sheet.

IBM Plex Mono, IBM Plex Sans, Instrument Serif
02

The fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it.

"155 milliseconds." Speed-led, dark editorial. Hero is a to-scale timeline from key-down to paste with the number as the biggest thing on the page; cyan is the only accent, used for timing marks; every later section carries an engineer's margin annotation with a time or size figure.

Geist, Geist Mono, Instrument Serif
03

Your voice never leaves the machine.

"Your voice never leaves the machine." Privacy-led but sober, laid out like a printed notice on cream paper. One serif with real italics and small caps, hairline rules, real file paths in the hero, and an on-disk or in-memory tag in the margin of every section.

EB Garamond, IBM Plex Mono
04

Show your agent. Stop describing your screen.

"Show your agent, stop describing your screen." A terminal-native page for people who run coding agents. The hero is a chat box where a take has just been pasted next to the folder tree the agent will open. Slate dark, one amber accent, IBM Plex Mono for structure and Newsreader for prose, box-drawing trees, a blinking caret.

IBM Plex Mono, Newsreader
05

Speak. The words land at your cursor.

"The pill." The whole app told through its one visible piece of UI, a large CSS reproduction of the overlay cycling idle, listening, locked, recording, transcribing, card at the foot of a blurred desktop; dark glass, cyan and red only, italic serif display type over mono labels.

Geist, Geist Mono, Instrument Serif
06

A day with it running.

"A day with it running". Six time-stamped scenes walk through one working day, each a CSS-drawn app window with the pill beneath it; daylight palette shifts from cool cream to evening amber section by section, set in Fraunces and Alegreya Sans like a magazine feature.

Alegreya Sans, Fraunces, IBM Plex Mono
07

It writes it the way you would have typed it.

"It writes it the way you would have typed it." A typographic ledger. The hero is a ruled before/after table of raw speech (serif italic) against what gets pasted (grotesque, tabular figures), leading with filler removal, number grammar and vocabulary before widening to gestures and recording. Light paper, Fraunces display serif with Instrument Sans, one oxblood accent, type as the only illustration.

Fraunces, IBM Plex Mono, Instrument Sans
08

The fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it.

"Free, MIT, Rust, one controller thread." A developer-facing page that opens with a CSS schematic of the app's threads and messages, then explains every feature as a consequence of that design. Graphite background, technical grid, labelled boxes with corner ticks, condensed sans labels, serif body, safety-orange accent.

Barlow Condensed, IBM Plex Mono, Source Serif 4
09

The old way took eleven steps.

"The old way took eleven steps." Two stacks of step cards in the hero, tall and cluttered on the left, three high on the right, then every feature gets the same old/new strip in miniature. Poster aesthetic, hot coral on chalk white and near-black, heavy geometric display type, thick outlines, stickers.

Familjen Grotesk, Unbounded
10

Speak, and the words land at your cursor.

The app as it lives in the Mac menu bar. A faithful CSS menu bar with the see.computer glyph and its frosted glass panel dropped down; every feature is introduced from the row, key, or overlay where you would meet it. Light, precise, Apple-adjacent, set in Schibsted Grotesk.

JetBrains Mono, Schibsted Grotesk
