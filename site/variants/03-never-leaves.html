A plain statement of what this app does with your voice

Your voice never leaves the machine.

see.computer is dictation and screen capture for the Mac. It is the fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it. Speak, and the words land at your cursor. Hold Shift too, and the screen and your voice are captured into a folder an agent can read. The speech model runs on your CPU. Free and open source, MIT.

Statement of record Every path below is real. Open them in Finder.
Audio

Read from the microphone while the trigger is held, then transcribed on your CPU by a model stored on this Mac. Dictation audio is not sent anywhere. When you record the screen, the audio of that recording sits in clip.mov inside its take folder, on your disk.

Text

Pasted at your cursor with Cmd+V. Kept as one markdown file per day unless you turn "Save Dictation History" off in the tray.

On disk
  • ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/models/The speech model, 670 MB

  • ~/Documents/see.computer/<stamp>/One take per recording
  • ~/Documents/see.computer/vocabulary.mdWords you want the model to favour
  • ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/history/History, only if macOS denies Documents access
Leaves the machine

One download of the speech model from Hugging Face on first launch, 670 MB. Nothing else. No analytics, no accounts, no other network calls.

Exception

If iCloud Desktop & Documents sync is on, macOS uploads ~/Documents/see.computer/ with the rest of your Documents folder. That includes transcript files and takes. The audio promise still holds. The text does not stay local in that case. Turn history off in the tray if that matters to you.

§ 1

Three gestures

One key does everything. Hold it to dictate, double tap it to keep the mic open, add Shift to record the screen. The default is Left Option, and you can change it in the tray.

in memory

Hold

Hold Left Option, speak, release.

Text is pasted at the cursor in any app. The model runs on your CPU while you talk, so the paste lands shortly after you let go. Audio never leaves the machine.

in memory

Lock

Double tap Left Option, speak, tap once more to finish.

A locked take for long narration, so you are not holding a key for two minutes. Tap the trigger to finish and paste. Esc throws it away. Inside a locked take you can hold the trigger again and add Shift for shots or clips. A lit ring appears around the pill, so a live mic never looks idle.

on disk

Record

Hold Left Option + Shift, release to stop.

Records the screen and the microphone while held. On release the recording is packaged into a take folder an agent can read. The paste is your narration in quotes, a blank line, then the names of the captures and the path to take.md. Release before about 0.6 seconds and nothing is kept.

in memory

Pick the trigger

Left Option, Right Option, or Fn (Globe), chosen in the tray. Right Option is AltGr on the Swedish layout, which matters if you type Swedish. Recording and locking follow whichever key you picked.

§ 2in memory

Fast because it is local

The speech model is NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6b v3, quantised to INT8 and run through ONNX on the CPU. Nothing waits on a network round trip, because there is none.

155 msA six-second Swedish sentence on an M3 Max
670 MBModel size, downloaded once on first launch
25European languages covered by Parakeet v3

The model is written to ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/models/ and read from there on every launch after the first. The README shows English and Swedish. Other languages in the set work the same way.

§ 3on disk

A take is a folder an agent can read

Hold the trigger with Shift and talk over what is on screen. When you release, the screen recording, its audio, the transcript and a set of still frames are written into one folder under ~/Documents/see.computer/. An agent that can read files can open the exact frames where each thing was said without decoding the video. A person, or a web chatbox, still gets the words.

Screen recording calls the system screencapture tool. There is no upload step. The folder is yours to read or delete.

§ 4

What is fixed before the paste

Three passes run between the model and your cursor. Each one has a rule you can predict.

in memory

Hesitation is dropped

Um, uh and hm go, along with the comma or full stop stuck to them, and the next word takes over the capital. "Um, so the deploy failed." pastes as "So the deploy failed." Like and actually stay, because they are usually real words. Er stays, because it is Swedish for "your". A dictation that was only a hum pastes nothing.

in memory

Numbers become figures

Ten and above are written as digits. Zero to nine stay as words unless they carry a percent sign or a decimal point. Works for English and Swedish.

on disk

Your vocabulary, one word per line

Put words the model misses in ~/Documents/see.computer/vocabulary.md. The file is re-read when it changes, with no restart. The words are pushed into the decoder while it is still choosing, not swapped in afterwards. A term only gets its bonus when the model was already considering it, so a long list stays safe. A bonus can win a close call. It cannot overturn a confident one.

§ 5in memory

The pill

A small overlay at the bottom of the screen. It tells you whether the mic is open, whether the screen is being recorded, and whether a take is locked, without a window to manage.

Motion is a spring with a 0.3 s response and 0.7 damping.

§ 6

The tray, the history, the trigger

The menu bar panel is native AppKit, drawn with NSGlassEffectView. It lists recent dictations and recordings. Click one to copy it again.

on disk

Dictation history

One markdown file per day in ~/Documents/see.computer/. Plain files you can read or delete. There is no auto-pruning, so the folder grows until you clear it. Turn saving off with "Save Dictation History" in the tray's Settings. If macOS denies the app access to Documents, the files go to ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/history/ instead.

Stated again, because it is the one hole in the promise. If iCloud Desktop & Documents sync is on, these transcript files and your takes are uploaded by macOS like anything else in Documents. The app cannot stop that. Audio is still never sent by the app.

§ 7

What macOS will ask for, and why

Four prompts. Each one maps to a single thing the app does. Nothing in this list is used for anything other than what is written next to it.

Accessibility

To press Cmd+V, so the text lands in the app you are looking at.

Input Monitoring

To see a bare modifier key being held down. Hold-to-talk on Option alone needs it.

Microphone

To hear you. The audio is fed to the model on your CPU and goes no further.

Screen Recording

For the Option+Shift video hotkey only. Dictation works without it. If you never record the screen you never need to grant it.

§ 8

Requirements and download

macOS 14 or later, on Apple Silicon. The DMG is on the releases page. There is no installer and no account.

in memory

How it is built

Tauri v2 and Rust. One controller thread owns the app state. No bundler, no Node toolchain. Screen recording uses the system screencapture tool. There are no analytics, no accounts, and no network calls beyond the model download.

on disk

Build it yourself

The bundle also ships a command line tool, useful for checking the model on a file without the hotkey.
