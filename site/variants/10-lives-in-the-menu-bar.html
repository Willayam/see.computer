Speak, and the words land at your cursor.

see.computer is the fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it. Hold a key and talk. Hold Shift too, and the screen and your voice are captured into a folder an agent can read.

⌥On the keyboard

Three gestures on one key

Everything starts on Left Option. Hold it, tap it twice, or hold it with Shift. There is nothing to open first and no window to find. The panel is only there when you want to look back.

option⌥hold, speak, release

Dictate

Text is pasted at the cursor in any app. The audio is transcribed on the CPU and never leaves the machine. Let go and the words are already there.

Hesitation and filler are dropped before the paste. Numbers come out as figures.

option⌥tap twice, speak, tap once to finish

Lock a take

For narration that runs longer than you want to hold a key. Tap the trigger to finish and paste. Esc throws it away. While locked, hold the trigger again and add Shift for shots or clips.

A locked take shows a lit ring around the pill, so a live mic never looks idle.

option⌥+shift⇧hold, release to stop

Record the screen

The screen and the microphone are recorded while you hold. On release the recording is packaged into a take folder that an agent can read, and the paste is your narration plus the paths.

Release before about 0.6 seconds and the take is discarded.

At the bottom of the screen

The pill says what the mic is doing

A small dark chip sits at the bottom of the screen while you hold the key. It has four states and no settings.

The row that is usually missing

Model recovery only appears when the model is unavailable

The rest of the time the panel has nothing about the model at all, because there is nothing to manage. Here is what runs under that empty space.

About 155 ms

On an M3 Max, a six-second Swedish sentence comes back in about 155 ms. The key goes up and the text is at the cursor before your hand is back on the mouse.

On the CPU, on this Mac

The speech model is NVIDIA Parakeet TDT 0.6b v3 as INT8 ONNX. Audio is transcribed locally and never uploaded. There is no account and no server.

The model is 670 MB and downloads once, on first launch, into ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/models/.

25 European languages

Parakeet v3 covers 25 European languages. The README shows English and Swedish, and the Swedish is why "er" survives the filler pass below.

Behind the see.computer Folder row

One folder holds everything it made

The Folder row opens ~/Documents/see.computer/. Takes, the daily dictation history, and your vocabulary file all live there as plain files. Nothing is in a database.

Made for an agent that can read files

The narration comes in quotes, then a blank line, then the names of the captures and the path to take.md. An agent that can read files opens the exact frames where each thing was said, without decoding the video. A human, or a web chatbox, still gets the words.

Screen recording uses the system screencapture tool.

Dictation history, and the switch that stops it

Each day of dictation goes to one markdown file in the same folder. There is no auto-pruning. Read them, grep them, or delete them. The Save Dictation History switch in Settings turns the writing off.

The audio never leaves the machine. The transcript files are ordinary files in Documents, so if iCloud Desktop & Documents sync is on, they sync like everything else there.

Between the mic and the paste

What gets fixed before it lands

Three passes run on every dictation. Two of them remove and rewrite. The third is yours to feed.

Hesitation goes

"um", "uh" and "hm" are dropped, along with the comma or full stop stuck to them, and the next word takes over the capital. "like" and "actually" stay because they are usually real words. "er" stays because it is Swedish for "your".

A dictation that was only a hum pastes nothing.

Ten and above become digits. Zero to nine stay words unless they carry a percent sign or a decimal point.

Your words, in vocabulary.md

Put the names and terms the model misses in ~/Documents/see.computer/vocabulary.md, one per line. They are pushed into the decoder while it is still choosing, not swapped in afterwards. The file is re-read when it changes. No restart.

A term only gets its bonus when the model was already considering it, so a long list stays safe.

In the panel itself

A panel that never takes focus

The panel is a non-activating glass view. Opening it does not steal the cursor from the app you were dictating into, so you can look back at a dictation and keep typing where you were.

Recent

Recent dictations and recordings are listed by their text. A waveform glyph marks a dictation, a video glyph marks a recording. Pick one and that text is copied again. For a recording that is the same narration-plus-paths paste as before.

Trigger

Left Option, Right Option, or Fn. Right Option is AltGr on the Swedish layout. Recording and locking always follow whichever trigger you picked.

Another dictation app on the same key doubles every input. Quit it, or give one of them another trigger.

Settings, inline

Settings expands inside the panel with the see.computer Folder shortcut, Launch at Login, and Save Dictation History. Trigger and Permissions open beside it. Model recovery appears only when the model is unavailable.

Behind the Permissions row

Four prompts from macOS, and what each one buys

The Permissions row lists all four and opens the matching System Settings pane. None of them is used for anything beyond the line next to it.

Accessibility

To press Cmd+V for you. Without it the text is still on the clipboard and the pill says so, so you paste by hand.

Input Monitoring

To notice a bare modifier being held. The trigger is Option or Fn on its own, with no letter, and macOS only reports that with this permission.

Microphone

To hear you. Audio is transcribed on the CPU and never leaves the machine.

Screen Recording

Only for the video hotkey. Dictation works without it. Skip this prompt if you only want to talk.

Asked the first time you hold Left Option with Shift.

How it is built

Tauri v2 and Rust. One controller thread owns the app state. There is no bundler and no Node toolchain, so the build is a single cargo command. Screen recording calls the system screencapture tool rather than shipping its own capture code.

The bundle also carries a CLI. see-computer transcribe <wav> transcribes a file, and see-computer clip <mov> turns a recording into a take.
