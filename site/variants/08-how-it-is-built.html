Free. MIT. Rust. One controller thread.

The fastest way to share what you are seeing and saying with your computer, and with the agents running on it.

Speak, and the words land at your cursor. Hold Shift too, and the screen and your voice are captured into a folder an agent can read. see.computer is a menu-bar app for the Mac, written in Rust on Tauri v2. This page shows how it is put together, for people who want to read the design before they trust it or fork it.

Design, then features

The features fall out of the drawing

Most of what makes the app pleasant to use is not a feature somebody wrote. It is a thing the design makes impossible to get wrong.

  1. Nothing slow runs on the hotkey thread

    The event tap decodes modifier events and sends a message. Transcribing, packaging and pasting each live on another thread, so a 670 MB model can never make a keystroke late.

  2. Dictating while recording cannot be expressed

    The state is an enum with one variant per activity. There is no variant for doing two of them at once, so that bug has nowhere to live. The compiler checks it, not a test.

  3. The pill cannot take focus

    The overlay window is created non-focusable and ordered in with orderFrontRegardless exactly once. It can never take the Cmd+V that pastes your words into the app you were using.

  4. The tray menu never becomes key

    The menu is a non-activating NSPanel filled with NSGlassEffectView, built in native/menu.m from a row list that tray.rs rebuilds on every open. The app you are dictating into keeps its focus while you change a setting.

  5. The lock dies with the tap

    A locked take is a flag inside the event tap that decodes the gesture. If macOS tears the tap down, the flag goes too and the take finishes as a normal release would.

  6. Every thread names its priority

    There is one file, qos.rs, where each thread declares its scheduling class. When the machine is busy, the tap, the controller and the paste still get scheduled before the model does.

Input

Three gestures on one key

The tap only has to tell apart a hold, a double tap, and a hold with Shift. Everything the app can do hangs off those.

01. Dictate
Left Optionhold, speak, release

Hold, speak, release. The text is pasted at the cursor in whichever app has focus. Works anywhere Cmd+V works.

02. Locked take
Left Optiondouble tap, speakLeft Optiontap to finish

For long narration when you do not want to hold a key. Tap once more to finish, or press Esc to throw it away. The pill shows a lit ring while locked. Inside a locked take you can hold the trigger again, and add Shift for shots or clips.

03. Record
Left Option+Shifthold, release to stop

Records the screen and the microphone while held. On release, both are packaged into a take folder and a short paste points an agent at it. Releasing before about 0.6 seconds discards it.

The trigger key is choosable in the tray. Left Option, Right Option, or Fn, the Globe key.

Engine

Local, and fast enough to stop noticing

Transcription runs on the CPU inside the app's own process. No audio leaves the Mac, because there is nowhere for it to go.

155 msA six-second Swedish sentence on an M3 Max, from release to text.
670 MBParakeet TDT 0.6b v3, INT8 ONNX. Downloaded once on first launch, resumable.
CPUONNX Runtime, no GPU and no network. The model runs inside the app's process.

Memory that the OS can take back

The weights are memory-mapped from ~/Library/Application Support/see.computer/models/. They stay clean, evictable pages, so a menu-bar app that is idle most of the day does not sit on 670 MB of dirty memory. The engine warms on key-down, before you have finished the first word.

A vendored crate, on purpose

Vocabulary boosting needs a hook that rescores the joint logits before the argmax. Upstream parakeet-rs does not expose one, so the crate is vendored with a small TokenBias trait added. The change is meant to go upstream. Until it does, the diff is in the repo where you can read it.

Recording

A take is a folder an agent can read

Hold the trigger with Shift and the app records the screen and your voice. On release it writes a folder with the video, the transcript, and JPEG frames, so an agent can open the exact frame where you said each thing without decoding the video.

Folder layout 
~/Documents/see.computer/<stamp>/
├── take.md
├── transcript.json
├── shots/
└── clips/

How the video is captured 
/usr/sbin/screencapture -v -g -x   # spawned on key-down
# …held…
kill -INT <pid>                     # on release

The system recorder does the work. The app only waits for it, on its own thread, and packages what it wrote.

The narration in quotes, a blank line, then the capture names and the path to take.md. Short enough to paste into a chat box, complete enough for an agent to go and look.

Why frames as well as video

An agent that reads a folder can open a JPEG. Few of them can seek inside a .mov. transcript.json carries the timing, the frames carry the pixels, and take.md ties them together.

Text

Cleanup happens before the paste

The model's output is edited on the way to the clipboard. Fillers go, numbers become figures, and your own terms get a thumb on the scale inside the decoder.

Filler

um, uh and hm are dropped. The next word takes the capital, so a sentence that started with a hesitation still starts with a capital letter.

Numbers, English and Swedish

"twenty-five thousand" becomes 25,000. "tjugo procent" becomes 20 %. Ten and above are digits. Zero to nine stay as words unless they carry a percent sign or a decimal.

Vocabulary

Terms from a file are boosted inside the decoder, not patched in afterwards. The boost is gated to candidates within SEE_COMPUTER_BOOST_MARGIN logits of the model's best guess, so a term cannot override a word the model was sure about.

~/Documents/see.computer/vocabulary.md 
# one term per line, re-read on change

Knobs

SEE_COMPUTER_BOOST_DEPTH    # how far into a term to keep pushing
SEE_COMPUTER_BOOST_MARGIN   # gate, in logits from the best guess

Environment variables. Leave them unset and you get the defaults the app ships with.

Menu bar

Tray, triggers, history

The menu is native code, rebuilt from a row list every time you open it. It reads as a small settings sheet and never takes focus from the app you are in.

Trigger

Pick Left Option, Right Option, or Fn, the Globe key. The choice changes what the event tap listens for. Nothing else in the drawing moves.

History

One markdown file per day in ~/Documents/see.computer/. There is an off switch under Settings in the tray. If Documents is synced to iCloud, your history is too. Decide whether you want that before you leave it on.

Under the hood

A non-activating NSPanel with an NSGlassEffectView, built in native/menu.m. tray.rs hands it a row list on each open, so the Rust side owns the content and Objective-C only draws it.

Permissions

What macOS will ask for, and why

Four prompts. Each one maps to a single thing in the drawing. If a prompt does not make sense to you, the mechanism next to it should.

PermissionUsed for
AccessibilityThe paste thread presses Cmd+V in the app you were using. That is how text gets into another app.
Input MonitoringA bare modifier key held on its own never reaches the normal shortcut APIs. The event tap needs this to see Option go down and come up.
MicrophoneDictation. Audio goes to the engine worker and nowhere else.
Screen RecordingThe screencapture waiter. Only the video gesture needs it, so you will not be asked until you use one.

If you build from source, macOS ties these grants to the signature. Set APPLE_SIGNING_IDENTITY before you build or you will be asked again after every rebuild.
